An example, not a family history

All people, places, sources and events in this report are invented. Nothing was drawn from a private family project or a public figure's biography. The two source texts were written for this demonstration on 12 September 2026. Dates inside them belong only to the imagined story.

The question is narrow: does the supplied material establish that Person P002 first lived in Place B in 1912? The answer is no. One source recalls 1912; another records a residence there in 1911. The permanent move date remains unresolved.12

Two sources, two different statements

First-hand recollection

The fictional Person P001 recalls childhood in an imagined 1950 interview. This is how the memory is expressed, not a verified transcript of a real recording:1

I remember moving from Place A to Place B with Person P002 in 1912. We started at the school together.

Person P001, invented recollection in 1950; E001, line 3.

Supported claim: the fictional speaker remembers a shared move in 1912. The supporting evidence is E001, line 3. There is no opposing evidence about what the speaker says. That does not establish the event date, and a memory described decades later can combine separate events.1

Source statement

The fictional register records Person P002 at Place B school on 4 September 1911 and lists Place B as the residence. It gives no parent, sibling, previous school or move date.2

Supported claim: the register lists a Place B residence in 1911. E002, lines 2-3, supports that statement; there is no opposing evidence about the register's wording. This is support for what a source says, not a blanket endorsement of its accuracy.2

What can we infer?

Disputed claim: Person P002 first lived in Place B in 1912. The shared-move recollection in E001 supports that reading, while the earlier residence in E002 opposes it. Both links matter. Choosing the more precise date simply because it looks official would not resolve the conflict.12

Tentative inference: Person P002 may have been resident in Place B by September 1911. E002 supports that possibility; E001's account of a shared move in 1912 challenges it. Temporary residence, a later reunion, a mistaken date or an incorrect identity link could explain the difference. None has been established.12

The P002 label joins the two sources by construction in this fiction. In real research, matching a name would not prove that they describe the same person. The supplied material also does not establish a family relationship between P001 and P002.12
