# Privacy, rights and sharing

There are three different decisions: sharing reusable software, processing family
data with a provider, and distributing a family report. None authorises the
others automatically.

## Separate the stores

This checkout is a generic starter. Keep real artefacts, manuscripts, transcripts,
indexes and findings in a separate private folder. The initializer does not
create Git history or remotes and never uploads anything. A private repository
is not a reason to put an entire family's data online.

Ignore rules help prevent accidental Git inclusion, but are not encryption,
access controls, AI content exclusions or provider-retention settings. Local
commands run locally; an AI assistant reading their outputs may still send
context to its service. Review the actual product/mode/account settings before
allowing private inputs.

Use the smallest necessary source extracts, redact living-person information
when appropriate, and obtain consent for recordings, cloud processing and
publication separately. Credentials, session URLs, cookies and connection tokens
do not belong in metadata, prompts, fixtures, reports or Git.

## Source and publication rights

Possessing a document does not necessarily confer reproduction rights. Record
custody, copyright/reuse terms, archive restrictions, narrator consent and any
recipient limits. An eulogy can include sensitive details about living people
other than its subject. Respect speaker review, access restrictions and
withdrawal arrangements.

Before sharing a report, review accuracy, uncertainty, privacy, consent,
photograph/document reproduction rights, accessibility and intended recipients.
Do not infer permission to publish from permission to research or from successful
technical checks. Login, MFA, purchases, contact with relatives and access-policy
changes remain human decisions.

## Software licensing and candidate review

The owner approved the [MIT licence](../LICENSE) on 2026-09-12 for the starter's
code, documentation, skills and fictional examples. It permits use, adaptation
and redistribution, including commercial use, with the required copyright and
permission notice retained and without warranty. Initialised private projects
receive the licence alongside the copied runtime. Dependencies retain their
own notices and terms; see [THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md](../THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md).

The software licence applies only within its stated scope, not to
acquired archive records, photographs, audio, transcripts, people facts or
publication rights.

Before committing or distributing a reusable candidate, inspect the entire
tree and intended Git history: prose, comments, defaults, templates, outputs,
binary metadata, dependency manifests and author identity. Machine pattern
checks alone cannot establish privacy. Keep any sensitive reviewer-specific
search lists outside the repository, tests, CI, logs intended for sharing and
history. Use only synthetic markers in committed tests.

Visibility changes, collaborator invitations, commits, pushing and public
distribution require explicit owner approval. Do not create a share archive or
copy another private repository's history.

For a public starter, keep examples entirely fictional and label the sources and
outputs accordingly. Renaming people from a private project is not synthetic
data. A public figure's life is real research too: it needs genuine sources,
rights review and a separate project, not invented records presented as history.
